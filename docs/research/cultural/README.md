# Cultural source packs (P3-02)

**Status: RESEARCH ONLY.** These packs list sources and what each supports. They contain **no guide text**, nothing here is marked reviewed, and nothing is in `content/`. Drafting is P3-03, and only after the owner approves the sources and the open questions below.

Covers the pilot festivals: [Diwali](diwali.md), [Ugadi](ugadi.md), [Navaratri](navaratri.md), plus shared [practical dorm-safety sources](practical-dorm-safety.md).
Cultural research is **separate from date verification** (see [../dates-2027.md](../dates-2027.md)). No source in these packs is used for a date, and no date source is used for a cultural claim. CalendarLabs and other holiday-list sites are not used here at all.

## How to read a pack

1. **Source table:** title, publisher, URL, type, access date, how it was read, and the claim IDs it supports.
2. **Claim map:** every candidate claim, in our own words, with the tradition or region the source attributes it to, the sources, and a support level.
3. **Flags:** claims that are disputed, over-generalised, belief-vs-history, single-source, or unsupported.
4. **Gaps and questions:** what still needs a source, and editorial decisions for the owner.

Support levels: **Strong** = two or more independent reputable sources agree. **Moderate** = one reputable source, or several that may share a base. **Weak** = one non-reputable or promotional source, or a source that says "believed/said to". **Unsupported** = we found no source; must not appear in a guide.

## Source rules applied (CLAUDE.md, PRD section 11)

- **Reputable categories used:** encyclopedia (Britannica), university and academic institution pages (Princeton, Brandeis, Harvard Pluralism Project), national-library publication (BiblioAsia, National Library Board Singapore), temple and religious-organisation pages (attributed to their tradition), primary texts in translation (Valmiki Ramayana, Bhagavata Purana, Markandeya Purana/Devi-Mahatmya), advocacy/education nonprofit (Hindu American Foundation, attributed). Newspapers and magazines (Hinduism Today) are used for context, with their type stated.
- **Not used as sources for claims:** Wikipedia (discovery only: a reference list to verify), calendar and panchang sites, holiday-list sites, SEO or recipe blogs, commercial pages.
- **Attribution:** each claim names the tradition/region the source gives it. "Unattributed" means the source did not say, and a guide must then write "in some traditions" or omit it.
- **Wording:** all claims below are paraphrased. Nothing is copied. At most a few words are quoted, in quotation marks, where exact wording matters (for example a source's own hedge).
- **Terms:** Britannica, Princeton, HAF, Himalayan Academy (Hinduism Today), SYDA, BhaktiBharat, BBT/vedabase and temple sites all assert copyright. We summarise facts and ideas in original wording, link to the source, and do not reproduce text or images. No scraping or bulk download was done; pages were read individually (some via a browser because automated fetching was refused by Britannica, Brandeis and Pluralism Project; this was ordinary reading of public pages).
- **Honest reading notes:** every page's read status is recorded. Where a page was only partly read or a tool returned a model-made summary, that is stated and the claim is marked accordingly. Search-engine snippets are **not** counted as reading a source.

## Cross-pack findings the owner should know

1. **The "Ayodhya lit lamps for Rama" account is traditional, not textual.** Valmiki's chapters on Rama's return (sargas 127 and 128, read in full) describe flags, flowers and scented water, not a festival of lamps. Britannica itself says residents are "said to" have lit lamps. Diwali guide text must attribute this to tradition and to northern India, not to Valmiki.
2. **Links from Puranic stories to Diwali are asserted by secondary sources, not by the stories.** The Bhagavata passages on Naraka, Govardhana, Lakshmi and Vamana-Bali (read) never mention Diwali or lamps. Britannica ties Naraka to the second day and Govardhana to the fourth.
3. **Navaratri's nine-night frame is not evident in the Devi-Mahatmya cantos read.** Cantos 82, 91, 92 and 93 mention autumn worship (canto 92) but not nine nights or Vijayadashami. Cantos 83-90 are unread. Britannica attributes a nine-then-ten-day battle to "legend".
4. **Days and counts are inconsistent across sources:** Diwali is "five days" in most sources but six in HAF's list; Navaratri's battle is nine or ten days depending on the source; Durga Puja is ten days in Britannica and five days in a temple blog.
5. **Food and fasting evidence is thinner than cultural-narrative evidence.** Navaratri has reasonable support (Britannica plus a temple page). **Diwali and Ugadi have almost none from reputable sources.** Dishes beyond ugadi pachadi and a general "sweets" are unsupported.
6. **Britannica was the strongest source for all three festivals, but it is one publisher.** Several "agreements" in the packs are Britannica plus derivative pages. Corroboration from a second reputable cultural, temple or academic source is a standing gap.

## Editorial questions for the owner (before P3-03)

1. **Britannica as the backbone?** It is the best readable reputable source. Is it acceptable that each guide's core summary rests on one encyclopedia plus attributed temple/institution pages, with a corroboration gap flagged, or do you want a second reputable source for each core claim before drafting?
2. **Food and fasting:** may Diwali and Ugadi guides carry only what is supported (sweets and sharing for Diwali; ugadi pachadi for Ugadi), with an explicit "not yet available" for other dishes, rather than commissioning more research now?
3. **Non-Hindu traditions at Diwali:** include a short, attributed note that Jains, Sikhs and some Buddhists observe Diwali with their own meanings (supported by Britannica, Princeton, Brandeis), or keep the guide strictly Hindu and link out?
4. **Controversial or sensitive customs:** the Britannica text mentions gambling (card games) as a luck custom, fireworks with pollution limits, and Durga Puja's soil-from-a-threshold custom. Omit these, mention with care, or mention only fireworks (with safety)?
5. **Voice for "why celebrated":** a short, attributed list ("In northern India... In southern India... In many families...") rather than one story as the reason. Confirm this is the approach.
6. **The Brahma-creation claim for Ugadi:** Britannica says "according to some Hindu texts". We could not verify any primary text. Use Britannica's hedged wording only, or hold the claim until a primary or scholarly source is checked?
7. **Housing-safety guidance:** each campus differs, and some ban LED string lights as well as flames. Is "check your housing contract; battery candles may be allowed" with named examples acceptable, or should the dorm guidance avoid naming specific colleges?
