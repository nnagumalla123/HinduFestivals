### Why it is celebrated and what it means

| ID | Claim | Region/tradition | Sources | Support | Flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | The name comes from a Sanskrit phrase meaning "row of lights", and the festival is broadly read as light over darkness (also good over evil, knowledge over ignorance). | Hindu, unattributed | D1, D4, D6 | Strong | Symbolism is interpretation, not a fact; present as "widely understood as". |

## 3. Flagged claims

- **Do not state as fact:** that Ayodhya lit lamps in Valmiki's account (C6); that the Puranic stories "are Diwali" (C7-C10); that Diwali is "the Hindu New Year" (C16); that it is exactly five days (C12).
- **Single-source or weak:** C4, C5, C10, C15, C17; the Newar Buddhist details (C11); HAF's claim that Sita is Lakshmi.
- **Conflicts between sources:** five vs six days (D1 vs D6); Newar Buddhist practice (D1 vs D4); Sikh history framing (D1 vs D5); month naming (D1 vs D4).
- **Not found:** reputable sources on regional foods, any fasting practice, Gujarati New Year, Tamil/Telugu Deepavali customs (oil bath, Deepavali marundu), Kali Puja details, Bali story link.

## 4. Candidate alias and basics notes (for the basics review, not published)

Names seen in sources: "Deepavali", "Dipavali", "Divali" (Britannica lists all three). These are spelling variants; the existing alias "Deepavali" is supported. The existing provisional deities ("Lakshmi", "Rama") match the stories above (Lakshmi, Rama, Krishna all appear); "Krishna" and "Kubera" would be additions. Any deity list must say which tradition associates which deity.
