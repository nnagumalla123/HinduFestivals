## Editorial decisions for the owner
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Why celebrate Janmashtami?.** no date appears; the observance day is disputed between traditions and stays in the date ledger (Smarta versus Vaishnava).
- **Krishna Janmashtami: The birth of Krishna.** Krishna’s later life (Radha, the Mahabharata, the Gita) is deliberately left out of this festival guide to keep it focused on the birth.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: A night vigil and a midnight birth.** “or swing” was removed (owner direction, revision 2): no source read in full names a swing, so the text says only “cradle”.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Fasting and the midnight vigil.** neutral fasting wording only, stated once in the guide (revision 2 removed the repeated disclaimers and the unsupported “many families do not fast at all”); no rule, no menu and no health advice.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Smarta and Vaishnava traditions: why the day can differ.** the specific reckoning rules (sunrise, Rohini nakshatra, Ashtami tithi and evening or midnight timing) were removed from the public text because they rest on two news reports that phrase them differently and no calendar authority was read. The text now says only, in general terms, that observance dates can differ by tradition, attributed to the Odisha report.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Smarta and Vaishnava traditions: why the day can differ.** no date appears. The Atlanta ledger keeps the Smarta and Vaishnava observances separate and the festival stays “disputed” there.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Dahi Handi: the pot of curd.** the court-limit discussion (age, height and safety measures ordered in 2014 and 2016) and every pyramid height were removed from the public text as outdated and conflicting. The text keeps only the general safety concern; Down To Earth (2016) supports that the lightest, youngest climbers go to the top and that injuries have occurred.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Dahi Handi: the pot of curd.** no injury counts, deaths or graphic detail are given. “Well-organized event that follows the local safety requirements” and “best watched from a safe distance” are common-sense safety wording with no source.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Celebrating Janmashtami away from home.** practical suggestions, not cultural claims; no college is named. The cradle and footprints come from Incredible India; the “well run” advice is editorial. Revision 2 removed the low-pot game suggestion (no source supports it as safe) and the repeated fasting line.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Why celebrate Ganesh Chaturthi?.** no date appears. “Many Hindus invoke him before starting a new task” follows Britannica’s statement that he is traditionally worshipped before any major enterprise.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Ganesha’s birth: more than one story.** the broken-tusk connection was removed (owner direction): the Shiva Purana chapter does not mention a tusk, and the only source is Britannica’s retelling. The beheading stays non-graphic. “Born of Parvati alone” follows the translator’s footnote and Britannica.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Modaks and sweets.** no fasting or vegetarian claim is made; no source read describes fasting for Ganesh Chaturthi.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: How the festival became a public celebration.** the Shivaji and nationalism claim was removed (Britannica alone states Shivaji used the festival to encourage nationalist sentiment; HAF only says “some historians believe” it became a public event in the 1600s and Free Press Journal says Shivaji “reinvigorated” it, so there is no independent corroboration of the nationalist motive). The Britannica clause that Tilak’s symbolism made the movement “more communal” was removed from the public guide. Brut’s mention of Hindu-Muslim clashes in 1893 stays out.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Environmentally responsible immersion.** the specific Central Pollution Control Board claims (the 2020 revision, the 2025 guidance, named materials and fines) were generalized, because the guidelines themselves (a PDF) were not read and the claims rest on two news reports. The home bucket-immersion steps and the pour-the-water-on-plants advice were removed; the guide now points readers to a temple or community organizer.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Celebrating Ganesh Chaturthi away from home.** practical suggestions, not cultural claims; no college is named. The small unpainted clay image and natural colors follow the sources. Revision 2 removed the dorm bucket-immersion and pour-the-water-on-plants instructions (owner direction); the farewell is left to a temple or community organizer.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Celebrating Ganesh Chaturthi away from home.** allergen wording (coconut or nuts) is a general caution; no source lists modak ingredients.
- **Vijayadashami: Why celebrate Vijayadashami?.** no date appears. This guide is a companion to the reviewed Navaratri guide (nine nights, Durga and Mahishasura) and the reviewed Diwali guide (the homecoming); wording was kept consistent with both.
- **Vijayadashami: Vijayadashami, Dussehra and Dasara: the names.** Dussehra, Dasara and Vijaya Dashami stay as search aliases, and this section now states the regional distinctions (Dussehra in the north and west, Vijayadashami and Bijoya Doshomi in the east, Dasara in Karnataka) so the aliases are not read as identical.
- **Vijayadashami: Vijayadashami, Dussehra and Dasara: the names.** consistent with the reviewed Diwali guide’s line “Dussehra, earlier in the autumn, celebrates the victory over Ravana. Diwali is the homecoming.” The Navaratri guide covers the nine nights and Durga Puja in more detail.
- **Vijayadashami: Rama and Ravana.** the guide does not claim that the epic establishes Ravana’s death on the tenth day. No source read says so (Britannica says only that Dussehra celebrates the victory), and the overview was reworded from “the day Rama defeated Ravana” to “when Rama’s victory over Ravana is celebrated”.
- **Vijayadashami: Durga and Mahishasura.** kept short on purpose because the reviewed Navaratri guide carries the story; the phrase “in more detail” refers to that guide and should be revisited if the guides are ever linked.
- **Vijayadashami: Ramlila and the burning of Ravana.** the ISKCON example of children shooting flaming arrows at a Ravana figure was removed from the public text. The HAF interview (a single speaker) stays in the pack and is cited only for the sentence that some communities remember both victories on the same day. The college guide gives the participation advice.
- **Vijayadashami: Mahanavami and Vijayadashami: tools, books, shami and apta leaves.** Mahanavami customs (Ayudha puja, Golu across Navratri) are now kept apart from Vijayadashami customs (shami puja, the Jamboo Savari, apta leaves). Britannica places Ayudha Puja on the ninth day of Navratri; the Mysuru official site lists Mahanavami with Ayudha puja and Vijayadashami with Shami puja.
- **Vijayadashami: Bengal, Mysuru, Kullu and more.** the Bengal custom of mixing soil from a brothel threshold into Durga’s clay (Britannica’s “Did You Know”) is omitted as unnecessary for a beginner guide, as in the Navaratri guide.
- **Vijayadashami: Celebrating Vijayadashami away from home.** practical suggestions, not cultural claims; no college is named. The firecracker caution rests on Britannica’s statement that effigies are stuffed with firecrackers; “behind barriers” and “do not light fireworks yourself” are common-sense safety advice with no source.
- **Vijayadashami: Celebrating Vijayadashami away from home.** the books-and-laptop suggestion is presented as an optional modern adaptation of Ayudha Puja (Britannica’s “objects of knowledge”), not as a documented traditional ritual; Ayudha Puja itself belongs to Mahanavami.

## Source-critical notes (kept out of the public text)
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Why celebrate Janmashtami?.** the overview rests on Britannica plus HAF, Brandeis and Incredible India (four publishers, all agreeing on the core).
- **Krishna Janmashtami: The birth of Krishna.** Britannica and the Bhagavata text (canto 10, chapter 3, read in full) agree on the sequence. The text read says the Lord appeared “in the dense darkness of night”; it does not use the word midnight. Britannica, HAF, krishna.com and ISKCON News place the birth hour at midnight, so the guide says “in the dark of night” in the story and “midnight” in the observance.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: The birth of Krishna.** the exact wording of the voice in the sky and the swap of the babies follows Britannica. The Bhagavata text read does not use the words “eighth son” in chapter 3; the prophecy is in earlier chapters, which were not read. Britannica and Incredible India supply it.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: A night vigil and a midnight birth.** bathing in water and milk, dressing, jhankis and prasada are Britannica; abhisheka, kirtan, dramas and readings are HAF; cradle and footprints are Incredible India; the conch shells and cradle are India TV; ISKCON News (2009) supports crib, tableaux and a public abhisheka for one tradition.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Curd, butter and sweets.** the sentence tying curd and butter to Krishna’s childhood stories follows Britannica’s explanation of Dahi Handi; no source names a specific home dish for Janmashtami, so none is named.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Mathura, Vrindavan and beyond.** Incredible India lists “Mumbai, Goa or Gujarat” for Dahi Handi; Britannica says “especially in Maharashtra and Goa”; the text keeps the two lists together without claiming more.
- **Krishna Janmashtami: Smarta and Vaishnava traditions: why the day can differ.** HAF’s explanation that devotees fast on the day before because ancient India counted a new day from dawn was also left out of this section, since it is a timing rule.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Why celebrate Ganesh Chaturthi?.** the overview rests on four sources (Britannica, HAF, Brandeis and Hinduism Today through the observances).
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Ganesha’s birth: more than one story.** one targeted attempt found and read a primary source: Shiva Purana, Rudra Samhita, Kumara Khanda, chapter 13, in J. L. Shastri’s translation on wisdomlib (read in full, verses 1 to 39 and footnotes). It supports the dirt of Parvati’s body, the companions’ advice, the gatekeeper with a staff, and Ganesha barring Shiva. The head-cutting appears in that chapter only in its opening frame (Brahma says the story varies by kalpa and that in one version Shiva cut off the head and an elephant’s head was put on), and the chapter ends before the confrontation is resolved. The later chapter on the head, listed in the index, was not read.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Ganesha’s birth: more than one story.** Britannica supplies the cloth version, the Saturn variant and the statement that he is traditionally regarded as the child of both; HAF supplies that stories vary on the elephant head. The translator’s footnotes also mention the Saturn variant and say Ganesha is born of Parvati’s body without Shiva’s intervention.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Ganesha, the remover of obstacles.** Britannica Ganesha (name, vehicle, remover of obstacles), HAF (the mouse as the wandering mind), Brandeis (success and happiness). “One of the most popular festivals” is Brandeis. The last sentence is a mild editorial summary.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Welcoming Ganesha.** nearly every detail is Britannica; HAF supports clay images, invoking Ganesha, the sixteen steps and visarjan as a return to Kailash; Hinduism Today supports daily puja, a simple farewell puja and a procession; HPI supports immersion on Anant Chaturdashi. Britannica says the longest observance ends “on the 11th day (called anant chaturdashi, or 10 days following the first day)”; HAF, Hinduism Today and Hindu Press International say “ten days”. Revision 2 states the counting convention explicitly (day one is the first day; Anant Chaturdashi is the eleventh day and ten days after the first).
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Modaks and sweets.** the number 21 is Britannica; a Mangalorean modak recipe in the Hinduism Today article is not used.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: How the festival became a public celebration.** the 1890s history is sourced to Britannica (Tilak, 1893, the ban on political assemblies), Brut (private before; Peshwa patronage ending in 1818; 1893 as the key year; Rangari possibly 1892; the disagreement; mandals) and Free Press Journal (Rangari “believed to be the spark”; Kesari). Brut and FPJ are news-media features, not scholarship; no academic history was read.
- **Ganesh Chaturthi: Environmentally responsible immersion.** the general direction of the guidance is supported by Britannica, the Tribune/PTI report (2020), Telangana Today (2025) and Citizen Matters (2024, uneven enforcement; clay fragile and heavy for large groups). The Hans India home-immersion article was dropped from the guide’s sources and stays in the pack as read and not used.
- **Vijayadashami: Why celebrate Vijayadashami?.** the overview rests on Britannica (two articles), HAF and Brandeis. “Each person can overcome their own faults” follows HAF (“the end of negativity and evil within us”) and Brandeis (internal enemies, vices).
- **Vijayadashami: Vijayadashami, Dussehra and Dasara: the names.** the name derivation, the interchangeable use and the Diwali link are Britannica (Dussehra); Vijayadashami, Vijaya Dashami and Bijoya Doshomi as names for the final day of Durga Puja are Britannica (Vijayadashami); Dasara is the Karnataka tourism and Mysuru sites’ own name for the festival. Britannica’s Diwali timing (“20 days after Dussehra”) is a date relation and is not used.
- **Vijayadashami: Rama and Ravana.** Britannica (Dussehra) tells the war and the Brahmastra; the Valmiki sarga 108 (Murthy) was read in full: Matali, the blazing arrow, Ravana’s heart. HAF and Brandeis give the festival link. UNESCO says Ramlila is based on the Ramacharitmanas by Tulsidas, composed in the sixteenth century.
- **Vijayadashami: Durga and Mahishasura.** the Devi Mahatmya attribution is Britannica (Durga Puja). The Navaratri guide notes that the Devi Mahatmya cantos read do not mention Vijayadashami; this text says only that Durga Puja ends on Vijayadashami and does not claim the Devi Mahatmya names the day.
- **Vijayadashami: Ramlila and the burning of Ravana.** Ramlila length, places and participation are UNESCO (read in full); Ravana dahan, the firecrackers and the effigies are Britannica; the inner meaning is Brandeis.
- **Vijayadashami: Mahanavami and Vijayadashami: tools, books, shami and apta leaves.** Golu and Ayudha Puja are Britannica (Dussehra); the Mysuru details are the official site and Karnataka Tourism (private Ayudha Puja for weapons and royal vehicles); the apta custom, the sona phrase and the Pandava and Maratha explanations are Outlook Traveller (a travel magazine, read in full), which ties the custom to “Dussehra” without naming a day of the festival.
- **Vijayadashami: Mahanavami and Vijayadashami: tools, books, shami and apta leaves.** Karnataka and Telangana “jammi” or “banni” leaf customs appeared in a blog and are not used.
- **Vijayadashami: Bengal, Mysuru, Kullu and more.** Bengal is Britannica (Vijayadashami); Mysuru is the official site and Karnataka Tourism (the official site counts eleven days and Karnataka Tourism ten, so no day count is given); Kullu is Britannica (Dussehra) only, since the Incredible India page was read only through a model summary. Revision 2 kept Kullu to one sentence limited to what Britannica says (no bonfire for Lanka, no start day).
