**RESEARCH ONLY. No guide text, nothing reviewed, no dates.** Batch A (P3-05), prepared 2026-11-08. Read [README.md](README.md) and [source-use-policy.md](source-use-policy.md) first. > **Revision note 2026-11-08 (owner decisions):** the draft keeps the pillar of light (Puranas tell the story; Shaiva festival tradition associates it with the night), keeps the poison and marriage accounts briefly, drops Parvati's fasting account, keeps the hunter and a one-sentence Herath, and makes no IGNCA permission request. Where this pack and the draft differ, the draft and the Batch A packet govern.

**Summary of the evidence.** The festival's core (a night of fasting and vigil with worship of Shiva, usually in the lingam form, in the dark half of a late-winter month) is well supported by Britannica, the Pluralism Project (Harvard), Brandeis and a government tourism page. The **origin stories** need care: each is supported differently, the primary texts do not link the best-known one (the pillar of light) to this night, and Britannica's own legend list sits only in an AI-generated summary page. The best source on the Kashmiri tradition (IGNCA, Delhi) **restricts hyperlinks to its site** (blocker B1). No source read mentions bhang or thandai for Shivaratri.

## 4. Distinctions requiring care

- **Maha versus monthly Shivaratri:** every dark 14th is sacred to Shiva; "Maha" is the late-winter one (SH1).
- **Origin stories differ and none is "the" reason.** Pillar of light (night link is a tradition, not stated in the Puranas read), poison, marriage, Parvati's fast, cosmic dance, the hunter (primary text). Present as "stories told about this night".
- **Kashmiri Shaiva observance (Herath)** is a distinct, fortnight-long tradition with walnuts and non-vegetarian offerings; never merge it into a general description.
- **Lingayat observance** is attributed to Lingayats.
- **Fasting and diet:** optional and variable; no vegetarian rule; Kashmiri Pandit practice includes fish and mutton offerings.
- **Shaiva, Smarta and Vaishnava contexts:** the festival is Shaiva by origin; do not say all Hindus observe it the same way.
- **No contemporary politics** arises in the sources read.

## 6. Recommended guide sections

1. **Why celebrate? (overview, 80 to 150 words).**
2. **Stories told about the night (story, attributed to each tradition):** the pillar of light (with the note that the Puranas tell it without naming this night), the poison and the marriage as "also told", the hunter from the Shiva Purana. Optionally Parvati's fast. Three or four short stories; each attributed.
3. **The night vigil and the lingam (traditions):** fast, vigil, abhishekam, chanting, bilva leaves; the Shiva Purana's four-watch vigil as a text.
4. **Fasting and food (food and fasting):** optional, varies, no vegetarian rule; the next day's feasting; Kashmiri non-vegetarian offerings if B1 is cleared; omit the sabudana list unless a second source appears.
5. **Kashmir's Herath (regional variation):** blocked by B1; otherwise a short attributed summary.
6. **Shiva across traditions (regional variation):** Lingayats' gifts to the guru, Nepal's Pashupatinath, and the American temples (nrityanjali, all-night vigils).
7. **Celebrating away from home (college guide):** campus or temple observances; no oil lamps or open flames (see [practical-dorm-safety.md](practical-dorm-safety.md)); a small lingam or image with water and flowers; being awake all night is optional and you should look after your health and class schedule (no medical advice); sharing a simple meal.
- **Quick facts:** Meaning; Length ("one night; the next day brings feasting"); Associated deities (Shiva; Parvati); Common customs; Dietary practices ("fasting is common but optional and varies; practices differ by tradition").
- **Deity entries:** Shiva (Shaiva and wider Hindu traditions); Parvati (the marriage and Brandeis stories; as attributed).
