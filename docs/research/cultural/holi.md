Three owner decisions bear directly on this festival: **bhang and thandai** (decision 1), **sensitive customs** (decision 3) and **contemporary controversy** (decision 2). A named-text check found that **Holika is not named in the Bhagavata Purana's Prahlada chapters read or in the abridged Vishnu Purana text read**, so the burning-of-Holika episode cannot be attributed to a specific text yet.

> **P3-06 revisions (2026-11-12):** F-03 the eye-safety tip now follows the American Academy of Ophthalmology page (re-read in full: chemical splash means flush immediately and seek emergency medical treatment right away; small particles usually not an emergency, do not rub, blink and flush, see a doctor if it still feels like something is in the eye); F-08 the HAF sentence now follows the page (understanding the origin can enrich the experience).

## 4. Distinctions requiring care

- **Two days, two characters:** the bonfire (Holika Dahan) and the colour day. Britannica ties the bonfire to Holika and the colour to Radha-Krishna.
- **Three stories, regionally weighted:** Holika and Prahlada (north, bonfire), Radha and Krishna (Braj, colour), Shiva and Kama (south). Never present one as "the" origin; HAF notes that some communities prefer one story.
- **The Holika episode's textual source is unverified.** Prahlada's story is Puranic (read in the Bhagavata and a Vishnu Purana text); Holika's role is not in those texts.
- **Regional forms range from two days to forty.** Lathmar Holi, Dol Jatra and Hola Mohalla are distinct observances.
- **Bhang versus thandai:** Britannica lists them separately: thandai as a sweet milk drink with nuts and spices, bhang as a mild cannabis-based drink. The guide must not blur them and must give no recipes or consumption advice. **Revision 3 (2026-11-08):** the guide now states only that sourced distinction (bhang "is listed separately from thandai and is served in some places"); the unsupported clause about cannabis-infused versions of other drinks and the inferred "ordinarily made without cannabis" wording were removed. The synthetic-color wording was also softened (ingredients vary by product; Britannica cautions that some pigments can irritate skin and eyes; some people choose herbal colors).
- **Hindu festival with wide participation:** HAF, Britannica and Incredible India all note participation beyond Hindus; keep religious roots visible.
- **Sensitive customs:** the norm-relaxing licence, harassment incidents and the widows' story need careful, non-graphic handling or omission.
- **No contemporary political content.** The Pluralism protest article was seen in a search and not used.

## 6. Recommended guide sections

1. **Why celebrate? (overview, 80 to 150 words):** spring, colour, a bonfire and its stories, two days.
2. **Stories behind the festival (story, each attributed):** Holika and Prahlada (no named text); Radha and Krishna; Shiva and Kama (southern). Plus Britannica's note on ancient roots (hedged).
3. **A bonfire and a day of colour (traditions):** Holika Dahan, then colour play, music, bathing, visiting.
4. **Colours then and now (traditions):** plant colours versus synthetic powders, Britannica's caution; no medical advice.
5. **Sweets and drinks (food and fasting):** gujiya, jalebi, malpua, puran poli (Maharashtra), chaat, lassi, thandai; bhang as a separate, cannabis-based drink, with one factual line and no advice (D1). **No fasting claim.**
6. **Holi across India (regional variation):** Braj and Lathmar Holi; Bengal and Odisha's Dol Jatra; the south's Kama Dahanam; Hola Mohalla as a Sikh neighbour.
7. **Holi beyond temples and homes (regional variation or customs):** participation beyond Hindus, appropriation and commercialisation as the sources frame them, and consent.
8. **Celebrating away from home (college guide):** attending a campus or community Holi; consent before colouring anyone; wearing old clothes; protecting eyes (editorial, AAO general guidance not Holi-specific); no bonfires (open-flame bans); respecting residence-hall rules on stains and cleanup; allergy awareness; no alcohol or drug advice.
- **Quick facts:** Meaning; Length ("two days: a bonfire evening and a colour day"); Associated deities (Krishna and Radha; Vishnu); Common customs; Signature foods (gujiya and other sweets, thandai).
- **Deity entries:** Krishna and Radha (Braj Vaishnava); Vishnu (Prahlada story, attributed); optional Kama and Shiva.
