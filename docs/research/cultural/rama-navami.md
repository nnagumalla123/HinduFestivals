**RESEARCH ONLY. No guide text, nothing reviewed, no dates.** Batch A (P3-05), prepared 2026-11-08. Read [README.md](README.md) and [source-use-policy.md](source-use-policy.md) first. > **Revision note 2026-11-08 (owner decisions):** the draft folds Ayodhya into the worship section, shortens the Michigan example, removes the inference that the festival arose later, keeps the spring Navaratri link and attributes the temple foods. Where this pack and the draft differ, the draft and the Batch A packet govern.

**Summary of the evidence.** The core (Rama's birthday on the ninth day of the bright half of Chaitra, the last day of spring Navaratri, with home and temple worship, Ramayana readings and, in South India, a ceremonial wedding) is supported by Britannica, Brandeis, the Pluralism Project and Hinduism Today, and the birth itself by Valmiki's text. The weak points are **regional specifics** (Bhadrachalam, panakam and other foods, differences between Vaishnava traditions), where only promotional or non-scholarly pages were readable, and **decision 2**: Britannica's article devotes a section to the festival's political dimensions, which the owner wants kept to a minimum.

> **P3-06 revision (2026-11-12, F-04):** Hinduism Today says devotees make and serve panaka and spiced buttermilk to the public; temples are mentioned only for sweets such as khoa. The guide now says so.

## 4. Distinctions requiring care

- **Birth versus festival:** Valmiki narrates the birth in Chaitra on the navami; the festival's practices are later tradition. State this as the pilot guides do ("the tradition says").
- **Regional wedding anniversary:** the Rama-Sita wedding on Rama Navami is a **southern** custom (Britannica, Hinduism Today); other traditions mark Vivaha Panchami (unsourced).
- **Chaitra Navaratri:** Rama Navami is its ninth day; the Navaratri guide covers the autumn Navaratri and says a spring one exists. The Ugadi guide already mentions Rama Navami in this context. Keep wording consistent across the three.
- **Vaishnava traditions differ** (Sri Vaishnava, Gaudiya, Ramanandi, Smarta): no reputable source read describes the differences; do not claim any.
- **Avatar framing** belongs to Vaishnava thought; attribute.
- **Contested Ayodhya history:** omit (D1).
- **Wedding sponsorship at a US temple** (families of the bride and groom) is a custom of one temple described in an undated essay; say "some temples".

## 6. Recommended guide sections

1. **Why celebrate? (overview, 80 to 150 words).**
2. **Rama's birth (story, attributed to the Valmiki Ramayana):** Dasharatha's sacrifice, the payasa, the four births; the festival itself is later tradition.
3. **A day of worship (traditions):** cleaning and bathing, the cradle, temple worship, readings, Ramlila, processions; fasting optional.
4. **The wedding of Rama and Sita (regional variation):** South India's kalyanotsavam; an American temple example; Bhadrachalam only if D4 is resolved.
5. **Spring Navaratri's ninth day (significance):** one short section relating the day to Chaitra Navaratri and cross-linking Ugadi and Navaratri.
6. **Food and fasting:** panaka and buttermilk at some temples; fasting optional and varied; **omit if only thin**.
7. **Celebrating away from home (college guide):** temples and student groups; reading a passage of the Ramayana; a small cradle with a picture of baby Rama; a battery lamp, not a flame; panaka is a simple drink (editorial; recipes are unsourced so give none); no dates.
- **Quick facts:** Meaning; Length ("one day, the ninth day of spring Navaratri"); Associated deities (Rama, with Sita, Lakshmana and Hanuman); Common customs; Signature foods (panaka, at some temples), or omit.
- **Deity entries:** Rama (Vaishnava and wider Hindu traditions; seventh avatar of Vishnu per Britannica); Sita; Hanuman.
