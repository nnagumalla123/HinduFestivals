### Food and fasting

C7 is the only supported food claim. U1 and U2 mention festive foods only through the pachadi; no source read describes other Ugadi dishes (bobbattu/holige, bevu bella and others appear in an unverified Wikipedia infobox only). **No source describes fasting for Ugadi.** Do not claim vegetarian practice; no source says anything either way.

### Regional variations

Supported: Telugu (Ugadi, ugadi pachadi), Kannada (Yugadi, bevu-bella), Maharashtra/Goa (Gudi Padwa). **Not supported by any source read:** Tamil Puthandu, Kerala Vishu, Punjabi Baisakhi and Sindhi Cheti Chand (which fall on other days or use other calendars) are new-year festivals often confused with Ugadi. The research pack has no cultural source for them; if the guide mentions them it must only say they are separate and fall on different dates, or omit.

### Away from home (practical)

Practical ideas to be developed in P3-03, not cultural claims: joining a Telugu or Kannada student association or local temple for Panchanga Sravanam or a shared meal (we found no named Atlanta organisation with a verified page; gap); making a simple version of the pachadi with whatever ingredients are available (no source for substitutions); video-calling family; rangoli on paper in a room (no open flame involved). A dorm may restrict hot plates; see [practical-dorm-safety.md](practical-dorm-safety.md).

## 3. Flagged claims

- **Belief vs history:** C4 (Brahma's creation) is a textual belief; the Satavahana/Shalivahana era link is unsupported.
- **Over-generalisation risk:** "the Hindu new year"; "everyone eats pachadi"; the exact six tastes; Panchanga Sravanam as "predictions" without saying it is a traditional almanac reading.
- **Single or secondary source:** C2, C4, C6, C7 rest mainly on Britannica; U2 is a popular magazine with no sources of its own.
- **Unverified references:** every print reference in section 1 except Narayanan's article's existence.
- **Date conflicts seen in search results (not cultural, noted only):** one source gave 2026's Ugadi as Mar 19 and another Mar 20. Dates stay in the date ledger and are not used here.

## 4. Candidate alias and basics notes (not published)

Britannica lists "Gudi Padwa" and "Yugadi" as other names, but Gudi Padwa is a **separate Maharashtrian festival on the same day**, not an alias. Existing alias "Yugadi": supported as a name (U1). The existing provisional deity "Brahma" is tied only to the hedged creation claim (C4), not to worship at Ugadi; if kept, it must say "in some texts". No other deity is supported.
