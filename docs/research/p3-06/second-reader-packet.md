# Optional second-reader packet (P3-06)

**Purpose.** A culturally knowledgeable second reader (a temple educator, a university chaplain, a scholar of Hinduism, or an experienced community member) reads the ten reviewed guides before wider publication. **Recommended, not required for Phase 3 completion** (owner decision, 2026-11-12). Please do not pass the guides to anyone as authoritative until the owner has decided what to do with the feedback.

## What to send

1. `ten-guides-reading-copy.md`: the exact public text of all ten guides (about 15,000 words), with no internal notes.
2. This page. A reader can finish in about two to three hours, or review only the guides closest to their own tradition (each guide is about 1,000 to 1,600 words).

## What the project is

A guest-first site for young Indian Americans and the diaspora: layered cultural education, traditional and dorm-friendly celebration guidance, and, later, reminders. It states **no festival dates yet** (all dates are "not yet verified"), cites sources, and keeps regional and sectarian differences visible. Stories are attributed to their traditions; religious narratives are kept separate from historical claims.

## Ten questions for each guide

1. **Accuracy:** is anything stated that is wrong, misleading or too confident for your tradition?
2. **Attribution:** are stories and customs credited to the right tradition, region or text? Is any custom presented as universal that is not?
3. **Omissions that matter:** is anything important to your tradition missing (not "everything", only what a beginner would be misled without)?
4. **Tone and respect:** does any wording feel dismissive, exoticizing, sectarian or too casual about the sacred?
5. **Sensitive material:** are the sensitive points handled with care (Ganesha's beheading, Mahishasura and Ravana as demons, Kali, Dahi Handi injuries, effigy burning)?
6. **Names and spellings:** are the names, diacritic-free spellings and aliases right (for example Makara/Makar Sankranti, Vijayadashami, Dasara, Gokulashtami)?
7. **Regional variation:** are there major regional or family practices that readers from other regions would be surprised not to see?
8. **Student guidance:** is the advice for students, away from home and in housing with rules, respectful and sensible?
9. **Fasting and food:** is the wording neutral enough (no rule, no health advice)?
10. **Anything you would change before a thousand people read it?**

## Points where your judgment is especially useful

| Guide | Why |
|---|---|
| Krishna Janmashtami | The Smarta and Vaishnava difference is stated only in general terms (no reckoning rules); Dahi Handi safety is stated without legal limits. Is that the right balance? Pushtimarg, Gaudiya and southern customs are not described. |
| Ganesh Chaturthi | The birth story follows the Shiva Purana version with other versions noted; the public-festival history (Tilak, Rangari, 1892 versus 1893) is hedged; immersion guidance is general. |
| Vijayadashami | Rama and Durga traditions are kept separate; Dussehra, Dasara and Vijaya Dashami are explained as regional names; Mahanavami and Vijayadashami customs are separated; Kullu is one sentence. |
| Holi | The Holika, Radha and Krishna stories; bhang named only as a separately listed drink; consent and eye safety. |
| Makara Sankranti | Pongal, Lohri and Uttarayan treated as related festivals rather than names for one festival; the solar and solstice explanation. |
| Maha Shivaratri | The pillar of light, the hunter story, Herath in one sentence; fasting wording. |
| Navaratri, Diwali, Ugadi, Rama Navami | Regional meanings of Diwali; Navaratri's three-phase and Durga Puja forms; Ugadi for Telugu and Kannada families versus other new years; Rama Navami and Ayodhya without politics. |

## How to respond

A short list is enough: guide, section, what you would change, and why (a source helps but is not required). The owner decides what to apply; any change goes through a new editorial revision.
