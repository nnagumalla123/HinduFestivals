# Cross-festival consistency matrix (P3-06)

Method: the term index (`term-index.md`, generated from the content files), targeted reads of every pair of related guides, and the claim ledgers. **Result: no contradiction between guides; two wording-standard inconsistencies (F-01, F-05) and a few S3 style points.** Nothing was changed.

| Related guides | What was checked | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Navaratri, Vijayadashami, Diwali** | Nine nights then the tenth day; Durga Puja's ten days and Vijayadashami as its last day; Dussehra as victory and Diwali as homecoming; Ayudha Puja on the ninth day (Mahanavami) | **Consistent.** Navaratri: "ends with Vijayadashami on the tenth day"; Durga Puja "traditionally ten days ... through Vijayadashami". Vijayadashami: "follows the nine nights of Navaratri", last day of Durga Puja, Mahanavami kept apart. Diwali: "Dussehra, earlier in the autumn, celebrates the victory over Ravana. Diwali is the homecoming." Navaratri's "Honor your tools" (ninth day, labelled a modern adaptation) matches Vijayadashami's optional adaptation. |
| **Rama Navami, Vijayadashami, Diwali, Ugadi, Navaratri** | Rama's description; spring versus autumn Navaratri; Chaitra Navaratri | **Consistent.** Rama is "seventh avatar of Vishnu" (Rama Navami), "hero of the Ramayana" (Vijayadashami); Rama Navami is the ninth day of spring Navaratri (Rama Navami, Ugadi); Navaratri covers the autumn one and mentions the spring one. |
| **Krishna Janmashtami, Holi, Diwali, Vijayadashami** | Krishna and Vishnu descriptions; Radha | **Consistent.** Krishna as an avatar of Vishnu in Vaishnava tradition (Janmashtami); Krishna and Radha in the Braj colour stories (Holi); Krishna and Narakasura in the south (Diwali). Radha does not appear in the Janmashtami guide (a scope choice, noted in its pack). |
| **Maha Shivaratri, Ganesh Chaturthi, Navaratri** | Shiva, Parvati and Ganesha | **Consistent.** Parvati as Shiva's wife in Shivaratri's marriage account; in Ganesha's birth story Parvati makes him in Shiva's absence; no clash. Navaratri's Parvati mention is a Durga/consort reference. |
| **Makara Sankranti, Ugadi, Holi** | Solar versus lunisolar new-year language; "Makara" versus "Makar" | **Consistent.** Makara Sankranti is the canonical name with "Makar Sankranti" an alias (search tested). Ugadi stays a lunisolar new year for Telugu and Kannada families; Holi's spring framing is separate. |
| **All ten: fasting wording** | "No universal fasting rule" and neutral wording | **Mostly consistent; one gap (F-01).** Janmashtami, Maha Shivaratri and Rama Navami state "Fasting practices vary by family and tradition." once each. Navaratri uses a longer form including "Many other families celebrate without fasting at all", which you removed from Janmashtami as unsupported. Diwali, Ugadi, Holi, Ganesh Chaturthi, Vijayadashami and Makara Sankranti make no fasting claim. |
| **All ten: housing and accommodations wording** | "Many residence halls ban open flames ... check your housing contract"; neutral accommodations line | **Consistent except Ugadi (F-05).** Nine guides carry "If you have questions about religious accommodations, ask your residence life office."; Ugadi's college guide does not. No guide names a college. |
| **All ten: tradition labels** | Every story and regional section has a `tradition` field; deity entries name the tradition | **Pass** (enforced by tests): no story or regional variation lacks a tradition. |
| **All ten: "optional modern adaptation" framing** | Practical ideas not presented as traditional ritual | **Consistent** after Batch B: Navaratri and Vijayadashami both label the books-and-laptop idea an adaptation. |
| **Name and spelling variants** | Navaratri (guides) versus Navratri (alias and some sources); Shivaratri; Makara/Makar; Ganesh/Ganesha; Vijayadashami/Vijaya Dashami | **Consistent**: one canonical spelling per festival in text; variants are search aliases; "Ganesha" for the deity and "Ganesh Chaturthi" for the festival throughout. |
| **Boilerplate sentences** | Repeated sentences across guides | Nine guides share the accommodations sentence; three share the open-flames sentence; two share "A video call with family ...". Intentional and consistent. |
